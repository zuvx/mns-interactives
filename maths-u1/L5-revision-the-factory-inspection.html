Inspection day

The inspector is coming

Today you check the whole factory. Every station you have worked at gets one last test, with new numbers.

This is practice, not the test. The real Progress Check is on paper and you do it on your own, with the iPads away. Nothing here is one of its questions.

Five checks:

  • Check 1 — finish the counting patterns.
  • Check 2 — the long runs, including one that goes past a hundred.
  • Check 3 — sort even from not even.
  • Check 4 — explain what even and odd mean.
  • Check 5 — estimate, then count.
Check 1 · Finish the patterns

What comes next?

Each line counts in a constant step. Work out the rule first, then the missing numbers.

Check 2 · The long runs

Keep going, and mind the corner

Longer runs. One of them crosses a hundred — watch the hundreds digit.

And backwards in hundreds

Check 3 · Sort the batches

Even, or not even?

Read the headings carefully. The second column says not even.

Check 4 · Explain it

Say what even and odd mean

The inspector wants explanations, not just answers.

On the paper you will have to write this in your own words. Practise saying it out loud now: an even number can be divided by 2 with nothing left over.
Check 5 · Estimate, then count

The last two jars

Look, choose a range, then group in tens and count for real.

Inspection complete

Ready for the Progress Check

Show your jar to your teacher. Then put the iPad away — the Progress Check is on paper and you do it on your own.

Before the paper, check yourself
Can you count on and back in 10s from any number, even past a hundred?
Can you count on and back in 100s?
Can you sort numbers into even and not even, and say why?
Can you give a sensible range for a jar you only glanced at?
