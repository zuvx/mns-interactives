Quality control

Nobody has time to count them all

Jars come past you fast. You cannot count every sweet, so you estimate. An estimate is a sensible guess — and a range is even better than one number, because it gives the answer somewhere to hide.

You will not be marked on being exact. An estimate is allowed to be a bit out. What it must be is sensible. Getting a sensible range is the skill.

How each jar works:

  • Press Look! The lid opens for a moment, then shuts again.
  • Choose a range you think the answer sits inside.
  • Then tip the jar out, group the sweets in tens, and count for real.
Station 1 · Sensible or silly?

Which estimates make sense?

Before you estimate anything yourself, judge somebody else's. Four workers looked at the same jar and wrote these down.

Think: an estimate that is a bit out is a good estimate. An estimate that could never be true is not an estimate at all — it is a wild guess.

In your maths book
1. Without looking, estimate how many pages are in your exercise book. Write it as a range. Then count the first ten pages, change your range if you want to, and finally count them all. Were you inside your range?
2. Write down three times you would estimate instead of counting, and say why counting would be silly each time.
