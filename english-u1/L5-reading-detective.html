Detective training

Six questions that catch people out

A reading question is not a memory test. The answer is nearly always in the text, and your job is to find it.

1. Who is telling the story?
2. Where are they, and how do they feel? Prove it with the words.
3. What does that word mean?
4. Which words tell us about a sound?
5. Who said this line?
6. How does the writer make it exciting?
Detective rule. Read the question twice. Underline the keyword. Then hunt for that word in the text before you write anything.
The story

The cliff path

I was halfway along the cliff path when the rain came. My boots slipped on the wet stones and I sat down hard. Below me the sea crashed against the rocks. I could not go back and I could not go on.

Then I heard it. First a low rumble, then a rattle, then a roar that filled the whole sky. An orange boat was cutting through the waves far below.

A woman in a yellow helmet climbed up towards me. Her rope scraped against the rock as she moved.

“Stay exactly where you are,” she called. “Do not stand up.”

She clipped a harness round my waist and pulled the strap tight.

“Ready?” she asked.

I nodded, although my legs were shaking.

“Then hold on to me, and do not look down,” she said.

The report

Now a real report about the same day

Storm rescue at Cove Bay

On Sunday afternoon the coastguard was called to Cove Bay, where four walkers were stuck on the cliff path in heavy rain. The lifeboat crew reached them in eleven minutes.

Before the crew climbed up, the coastguard spoke to the walkers through a loudspeaker. “Stay together,” she said. “Sit down. Keep away from the edge. Wait for us.”

All four walkers were brought down safely. Nobody was hurt, although one walker was taken to hospital to be checked.
